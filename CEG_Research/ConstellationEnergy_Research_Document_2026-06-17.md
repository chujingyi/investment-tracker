## 1. COMPANY OVERVIEW

Constellation Energy Corporation (NASDAQ: CEG) is the largest private-sector power producer in the world and the United States' preeminent clean energy company. Headquartered in Baltimore, Maryland, Constellation controls approximately 55 gigawatts (GW) of total generating capacity following its landmark $16.4 billion acquisition of Calpine Corporation in January 2026 — a fleet capable of powering the equivalent of 27 million American homes and supplying roughly 10% of the nation's clean electricity.

At the core of Constellation's identity and competitive moat is its nuclear fleet: 22.6 GW of capacity spread across 21 reactors at 12 sites, making it the single largest nuclear power operator in the United States by a substantial margin. Nuclear energy provides 24/7 carbon-free baseload electricity, a combination of reliability and emissions profile that no other generation technology can currently match at scale. This has made Constellation the indispensable energy partner of choice for hyperscale technology companies seeking to power artificial intelligence infrastructure with verifiable clean power.

Beyond nuclear, the Calpine acquisition layered in a substantial natural gas portfolio — Calpine was itself the largest gas-fired power producer in the United States — along with renewable assets including wind, solar, hydro, and geothermal generation. The combined enterprise spans five operating segments: Mid-Atlantic, Midwest, New York, ERCOT (Texas), and Other Power Regions.

Constellation's business model operates on two interconnected pillars. First, the company sells wholesale electricity into competitive power markets (PJM, MISO, NYISO, ERCOT) where its low-marginal-cost nuclear and gas fleet captures energy and capacity revenues. Second, the company serves as a leading competitive retail energy supplier, reaching approximately 2.5 million customer accounts nationwide — including contracts with 80% of the Fortune 100 — through commercial and industrial (C&I) retail supply, government energy contracts, and long-term power purchase agreements (PPAs) with technology giants such as Microsoft and Meta Platforms.

The company's 2025 revenue reached $25.53 billion, an increase of 8.34% compared to $23.57 billion in 2024. Q1 2026 revenue surged 64% year-over-year to $11.1 billion, reflecting the Calpine consolidation. Management has affirmed 2026 full-year adjusted operating EPS guidance of $11.00–$12.00 per share, representing a dramatic step up from the $3.40 EPS level when the company went public in 2022.

Constellation employs approximately 16,500 people as of early 2026, a workforce that spans nuclear engineers and operators, natural gas plant personnel, retail energy traders, and technology professionals. The company is structured as a competitive merchant generator — it does not own regulated utility distribution wires — which means its earnings are tied to wholesale power prices, capacity market payments, and the terms of long-term PPAs, as well as the federal nuclear Production Tax Credit (PTC) under Section 45U of the Internal Revenue Code.

The emergence of generative artificial intelligence has created a structural tailwind that is reshaping Constellation's competitive position. AI data centers require enormous, continuous quantities of electricity — and hyperscalers have discovered that nuclear power's "always-on" characteristics make it uniquely suited for this application. Constellation's position as the nation's dominant nuclear operator places it at the intersection of the most consequential energy and technology trends of the decade.

## 2. COMPANY HISTORY

### Origins: Baltimore Gas & Electric and the Roots of American Utility Power

Constellation Energy's roots extend back to the early 19th century through predecessor companies involved in gas lighting and electric utilities in Baltimore, Maryland. The institutional ancestor most directly relevant to the modern company is Baltimore Gas and Electric (BGE), which became one of the mid-Atlantic region's foundational electric utilities over more than a century of operations. BGE built and operated nuclear plants at Calvert Cliffs in Calvert County, Maryland — two 900 MW pressurized water reactors that became flagship assets of what would eventually become Constellation.

### 1999: Deregulation Creates Constellation Energy Group

The pivotal early chapter in the modern Constellation story was the wave of electric utility deregulation that swept through the United States in the late 1990s. As states moved to open power generation to competition, vertically integrated utilities were pressed to separate their generation businesses from regulated wires companies. In 1999, BGE's parent company restructured, separating its competitive generation operations into a new entity called Constellation Energy Group — a publicly traded merchant generator focused on nuclear and fossil fuel generation, with an associated retail energy business. This restructuring positioned Constellation as an early pioneer in the competitive merchant generation model.

Throughout the early 2000s, Constellation expanded its nuclear operations through strategic acquisitions and partnerships. The company acquired additional nuclear assets, built out its retail energy supply business, and developed expertise in the complex hedging and risk management required to operate a large merchant power fleet in competitive markets. By the mid-2000s, Constellation operated one of the largest nuclear fleets in the nation, with flagship facilities including Calvert Cliffs in Maryland, Nine Mile Point in New York, and Quad Cities in Illinois.

### 2009–2012: Attempted Sale to MidAmerican, Then Merger with Exelon

In 2008–2009, amid the global financial crisis and stress in power markets, Constellation entered negotiations with MidAmerican Energy (controlled by Warren Buffett's Berkshire Hathaway) for a potential sale. Those negotiations ultimately broke down. Subsequently, in 2012, Constellation Energy Group was acquired by Exelon Corporation for approximately $7.9 billion in stock, creating one of the largest and most complex utility conglomerates in the United States. Under Exelon's ownership, Constellation's nuclear and retail energy operations were combined with Exelon's existing nuclear portfolio — which included Braidwood, Byron, Dresden, and other Midwestern plants — creating by far the largest nuclear operator in the country.

The Exelon years were marked by operational integration, rising low-cost renewable competition (particularly wind and solar in the Midwest), and significant pressure on nuclear economics in energy-only markets. Exelon repeatedly threatened to close economically marginal nuclear plants unless state zero-emission credit (ZEC) programs were established to compensate for nuclear's carbon-free attributes. State programs in Illinois, New York, New Jersey, and Pennsylvania were created in large part as a direct response to Exelon-Constellation advocacy, establishing a critical policy precedent for nuclear support.

### February 1, 2022: Spinoff from Exelon — Constellation Becomes Independent

The most transformative event in the modern company's history occurred on February 1, 2022, when Exelon completed the separation of its competitive generation and retail energy business into a newly independent publicly traded company: Constellation Energy Corporation (NASDAQ: CEG). This spinoff created two distinct entities: Constellation, focused on competitive power generation and retail energy supply; and Exelon, retaining regulated utility operations (ComEd in Illinois, PECO in Pennsylvania, BGE in Maryland, and others).

Constellation launched as the largest producer of carbon-free electricity in the United States, with approximately 33 GW of nuclear and renewable generating capacity and the largest retail energy supply operation in the country. The company's stock began trading on February 2, 2022, and the spinoff was structured to position Constellation to benefit from growing policy and market demand for clean energy.

### 2022–2024: Policy Tailwinds and Nuclear Renaissance

The Inflation Reduction Act (IRA), signed into law in August 2022, represented a landmark policy development for Constellation. The IRA introduced Section 45U of the Internal Revenue Code, the nuclear PTC, which provides existing nuclear plants a production tax credit of up to $15 per megawatt-hour (MWh) of electricity generated, subject to profitability thresholds. This credit provided a significant earnings floor for Constellation's nuclear fleet, reducing earnings volatility and enabling longer-term capital commitments. Analysts widely credited the 45U PTC with enabling the rerating of CEG stock from a volatile merchant generator to a more predictable compounder.

In September 2023, Constellation announced a landmark partnership with Microsoft — a 20-year PPA to restart Three Mile Island Unit 1, a 835 MW nuclear reactor in Middletown, Pennsylvania, that had been shut down in 2019 for economic reasons. Renamed the Christopher M. Crane Clean Energy Center (in honor of Constellation's late CEO Chris Crane, who championed nuclear energy), the restart represented the first major nuclear plant restart in U.S. history and validated the concept of tech companies directly financing nuclear generation to meet clean energy goals.

### 2025: Calpine Acquisition Announced and Meta PPA Signed

In January 2025, Constellation announced its most audacious strategic move yet: a $26.6 billion agreement to acquire Calpine Corporation, the largest natural gas power producer in the United States. The deal would transform Constellation from a predominantly nuclear company into a diversified, technology-focused clean energy powerhouse with approximately 55 GW of total generating capacity. In June 2025, Meta Platforms signed a 20-year PPA for 1,121 MW of nuclear generation from the Clinton Clean Energy Center in Illinois, further cementing Constellation's role as the nuclear power partner of choice for hyperscalers.

In November 2025, Constellation also inked a more than $1 billion government contract to supply nuclear electricity and energy-saving technologies to federal buildings across the United States.

### January 7, 2026: Calpine Acquisition Closes

After receiving regulatory approvals from the New York State Public Service Commission, PUCT of Texas, and the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission, Constellation formally closed the Calpine acquisition on January 7, 2026, for a total consideration of approximately $16.4 billion. The combination created the world's largest private-sector power producer and dramatically expanded Constellation's natural gas capacity, customer relationships, and geographic reach — particularly in Texas (ERCOT) and California, markets where Calpine had dominant positions.

## 4. PRODUCTS & SERVICES

### Nuclear Generation — The Core Asset

Constellation's foundational product is carbon-free electricity generated by its nuclear fleet. With 22.6 GW of nuclear capacity across 21 reactors at 12 sites, Constellation's nuclear portfolio is effectively a natural monopoly in the U.S. nuclear space — no other private company controls anything close to this scale of operating nuclear assets.

Key nuclear sites include:
- **Calvert Cliffs** (Maryland) — 2 units, ~1,780 MW — flagship mid-Atlantic asset
- **Nine Mile Point** (New York) — 2 units, ~1,780 MW — critical New York grid asset
- **Quad Cities** (Illinois) — 2 units, ~1,784 MW — key Midwest baseload
- **Braidwood** (Illinois) — 2 units, ~2,386 MW — largest single site
- **Byron** (Illinois) — 2 units, ~2,347 MW
- **Clinton** (Illinois) — 1 unit, ~1,121 MW — subject of Meta 20-year PPA
- **Peach Bottom** (Pennsylvania) — 2 units, ~2,768 MW — mid-Atlantic powerhouse

Constellation is actively investing in nuclear uprates (increasing output of existing reactors through engineering upgrades) and has announced approximately 1 GW of planned nuclear uprates as part of its capital program. Uprates provide the highest-return capital deployment opportunity available to the company, as they increase output from already-operating, largely depreciated assets with minimal incremental cost.

### Three Mile Island / Crane Clean Energy Center Restart

The Christopher M. Crane Clean Energy Center (formerly Three Mile Island Unit 1) represents perhaps the most significant new nuclear project in the United States in decades. The 835 MW plant was shut down in 2019 for economic reasons. Constellation announced in September 2023 that it would restart the plant under a 20-year PPA with Microsoft, backed by a $1 billion DOE loan guarantee approved in November 2025. The plant is expected to begin generating power in 2027, making it the first nuclear restart in U.S. history.

The significance of the Crane restart extends well beyond its 835 MW capacity. It established a template for technology companies to directly finance nuclear power restarts and is expected to catalyze additional such deals across the industry.

### Natural Gas Generation (Post-Calpine)

Following the January 2026 Calpine acquisition, Constellation's generation mix expanded dramatically to include Calpine's roughly 26 GW of natural gas-fired capacity — primarily efficient combined-cycle gas turbine (CCGT) plants concentrated in California, Texas, and the Southeast. These gas assets provide:

- **Complementary baseload and peaking capacity** alongside the nuclear fleet
- **Geographic diversification** into markets (ERCOT, California, Southeast) where Constellation previously had limited presence
- **Incremental retail and C&I supply opportunities** in new markets
- **Operational flexibility** to capture high-price periods when power prices spike

Calpine was itself the most efficient large gas generator in the United States, with a fleet weighted toward efficient combined-cycle technology. This aligns with Constellation's emphasis on reliability and cost discipline.

### Retail Energy Supply

Constellation is the largest competitive retail energy supplier in the United States, serving approximately 2.5 million customer accounts including:
- **Commercial & Industrial (C&I) customers** — the largest revenue segment, serving manufacturers, hospitals, universities, government agencies, and data centers
- **Small Commercial customers** — local businesses across deregulated states
- **Residential customers** — households in competitive retail markets

The retail business provides Constellation with a natural hedge: the company generates electricity from its nuclear and gas fleet, sells it to the wholesale market, and simultaneously supplies power to retail customers — capturing the margin between wholesale and retail prices while managing the complex commodity risk exposures involved.

### Power Purchase Agreements (PPAs)

Long-term PPAs have become one of Constellation's most strategically important product categories. Key agreements include:

- **Microsoft / Crane Clean Energy Center** — 20-year PPA for 835 MW of nuclear power from the restarted Three Mile Island Unit 1, commencing expected 2027. This deal is the anchor for the entire TMI restart project.
- **Meta / Clinton Clean Energy Center** — 20-year PPA for 1,121 MW of nuclear power from the Clinton plant in Illinois, signed June 2025. One of the largest clean energy deals in history.
- **U.S. Federal Government** — More than $1 billion contract signed January 2025 to supply nuclear electricity and energy efficiency technologies to federal buildings nationwide.

Constellation maintains long-term power agreements with more than 180 Fortune 500 companies, representing approximately $3.8 billion in annual contract value. These locked-in revenues provide earnings visibility that distinguishes Constellation from pure merchant generators.

### Constellation Offsite Renewables (CORe)

The CORe program enables Constellation's C&I retail customers to meet their renewable energy goals through bundled energy products that combine power supply with renewable energy certificates (RECs) or direct investment in new renewable projects. This product line positions Constellation to serve corporate sustainability teams alongside their procurement and finance functions.

### Energy Management Services

Beyond power supply, Constellation offers energy management and efficiency services — helping C&I customers reduce consumption, optimize demand response, and manage energy budgets. These services increase customer stickiness, improve retention, and provide additional revenue streams beyond commodity electricity supply.

Constellation's go-to-market strategy operates on two fundamentally different tracks:

**Wholesale / PPA Channel**: Direct engagement with hyperscalers, government agencies, and major corporations requires a sophisticated long-term structured energy team. These deals are typically originated through senior executive relationships (CEO-to-CEO), supported by structured finance, legal, and regulatory teams that can negotiate complex multi-decade agreements. The sales cycle is measured in months to years, with contract values in the billions of dollars.

**Retail C&I Channel**: Constellation's retail energy supply force covers deregulated markets across the Northeast, Mid-Atlantic, and Midwest. **Key Partnerships**: Constellation works with EDF (the French state nuclear company), which holds a 49.99% interest in certain nuclear assets, as well as equipment suppliers like Westinghouse and GE Hitachi for nuclear fuel and services.

## 6. INDUSTRY OVERVIEW

### Defining the Industry: Merchant Power Generation and Nuclear Energy

Constellation operates at the intersection of several industry classifications: independent power production (IPP), nuclear energy, competitive retail energy supply, and increasingly, technology infrastructure. The most useful frame for understanding Constellation's industry context is the U.S. competitive wholesale electricity market, specifically the organized markets operated by Regional Transmission Organizations (RTOs) such as PJM Interconnection (serving the Mid-Atlantic and Midwest), MISO, NYISO, and ERCOT.

In these markets, generators compete to sell electricity at prices set by supply and demand. Unlike regulated utilities — which earn a guaranteed rate of return on invested capital — competitive generators like Constellation live and die by the economics of their fleet relative to market prices. This makes capital discipline, fleet efficiency, and long-term contracting (PPAs) critical to sustainable earnings.

### The Nuclear Energy Sector

The United States operates approximately 93 commercial nuclear reactors at 54 plants, generating around 100 GW of capacity — providing approximately 19% of total U.S. electricity generation. Nuclear is the largest single source of carbon-free electricity in the country, generating more clean electricity than wind and solar combined.

For decades following the Three Mile Island accident in 1979 and the Chernobyl disaster in 1986, new nuclear construction in the United States was essentially halted. The industry entered a prolonged period of decline, with no new plants ordered and several aging reactors closed due to economics. The emergence of low-cost natural gas (from the shale revolution) and falling costs for wind and solar put additional pressure on existing nuclear plants in energy-only markets.

However, the nuclear industry is now experiencing a genuine renaissance, driven by several converging factors:

**Energy Security and Reliability**: The global energy crisis of 2021–2023, exacerbated by Russia's invasion of Ukraine, demonstrated the strategic importance of domestic baseload power that does not depend on fuel imports. Nuclear's domestic fuel cycle (domestic uranium enrichment, domestically fabricated fuel) positions it favorably from an energy security perspective.

**Climate Policy**: Decarbonization targets at the federal and state level have elevated the value of 24/7 carbon-free generation. Nuclear power — which generates no CO2 during operation — is increasingly recognized by policymakers and corporate sustainability officers as an essential component of a zero-carbon grid.

**AI-Driven Electricity Demand**: This is the most immediate and commercially significant driver for Constellation. Generative AI training and inference require enormous, continuous quantities of electricity. A single large language model training run can consume as much electricity as a small city. As hyperscalers build out AI infrastructure, they have concluded that nuclear power's unique combination of 24/7 availability, carbon-free generation, and long-term price certainty is uniquely suited to anchor data center energy strategies.

### Market Size and Growth

**U.S. Power Market**: The U.S. electricity market represents approximately $450–500 billion in annual revenue. The competitive wholesale segment (where Constellation operates) represents roughly $150–200 billion of this, with retail energy adding further market exposure.

**AI Data Center Power Demand**: The growth trajectory of AI data center electricity demand is extraordinary. U.S. data center power capacity is forecast to rise from approximately 33 GW in 2024 to 41 GW in 2025, before potentially tripling to 120 GW by 2030 and rising further to 176 GW by 2035. Goldman Sachs estimates that data center electricity demand could rise by 160% by 2030. Federal Energy Regulatory Commission (FERC) data shows U.S. data center electricity demand is expected to climb from 19 GW in 2023 to 35 GW in 2030.

**Global Nuclear Market**: The global nuclear energy market is valued at approximately $400+ billion when including fuel cycle, construction, and services. The World Nuclear Association projects that global nuclear capacity could double from 400 GW today to 800 GW by 2050 as countries seek reliable, carbon-free baseload power.

### Regulatory Environment

Constellation's operations are shaped by several layers of regulation:

**Nuclear Regulatory Commission (NRC)**: The NRC governs all aspects of nuclear plant licensing, operations, safety, and decommissioning. Plant operating licenses must be renewed periodically (20-year extensions are typical), and the NRC's regulatory framework is among the most rigorous in any U.S. industry.

**Federal Energy Regulatory Commission (FERC)**: FERC oversees wholesale electricity markets, transmission access, and interstate electricity trade. FERC market rules directly impact the revenues Constellation earns in capacity markets (such as PJM's Capacity Performance market) and energy markets.

**Section 45U Nuclear Production Tax Credit**: The Inflation Reduction Act's nuclear PTC provides up to $15/MWh for eligible nuclear generation, subject to a profitability test (credits phase out as power prices rise above a threshold). This credit provides a meaningful earnings floor and reduces Constellation's exposure to low power price scenarios.

**State Policies**: Multiple states (Illinois, New York, New Jersey, Pennsylvania) have established Zero Emission Credit programs or equivalent mechanisms that provide additional support for nuclear generation within their borders, reflecting state-level clean energy mandates.

**Trump Administration Energy Policy**: The Trump administration (returned to office in January 2025) issued executive orders aimed at expanding domestic nuclear capacity from ~100 GW in 2024 to 400 GW by 2050, and modernizing NRC regulatory frameworks to accelerate licensing and reduce compliance costs. This policy environment is broadly favorable to Constellation.

## 7. COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE

### Overview

Constellation's competitive landscape is defined by its nuclear dominance in the United States, but the company faces meaningful competition in retail energy supply, natural gas generation, and the emerging market for long-term clean power agreements. Key competitors span regulated utilities with nuclear assets, pure-play merchant generators, and renewable energy companies.

### 1. Vistra Corp (NYSE: VST) — Primary Direct Rival

Vistra is Constellation's most direct and formidable competitor. Following its acquisition of Energy Harbor (completed 2023), Vistra owns four nuclear power plants: Comanche Peak (Texas, ~2,400 MW), Beaver Valley (Pennsylvania, ~1,800 MW), Davis-Besse (Ohio, ~900 MW), and Perry (Ohio, ~1,300 MW) — approximately 6.4 GW of nuclear capacity in total. While dwarfed by Constellation's 22.6 GW nuclear fleet, Vistra's nuclear assets give it credibility in the AI/data center clean energy conversation.

Vistra also operates a large natural gas and coal portfolio and has a competitive retail electricity business in Texas (TXU Energy) and other deregulated markets. With a combined fleet of approximately 40 GW, Vistra is the second-largest competitive power producer in the United States. Analysts project Vistra's two-year revenue CAGR at approximately 18%, slightly above Constellation's ~15%, but Vistra's smaller nuclear fleet limits its ability to compete for the largest hyperscaler PPAs. Vistra's competitive advantage lies in its dominant Texas market position and its flexibility across multiple fuel types.

### 2. NRG Energy (NYSE: NRG)

NRG Energy is a leading competitive retail electricity and power generation company, primarily focused on natural gas and retail consumer markets. NRG serves millions of residential and commercial customers under brands including Reliant, Green Mountain Energy, and others. NRG is attempting to position itself as a data center energy partner, but its lack of nuclear assets is a significant disadvantage in competing for hyperscaler long-term PPAs that specify carbon-free, always-on power. NRG's retail scale and marketing capabilities are competitive with Constellation in residential and small commercial segments, but the company lacks Constellation's nuclear moat for large C&I and technology customers.

### 3. Talen Energy (NASDAQ: TLN)

Talen Energy is a smaller independent power producer with a mixed portfolio of nuclear, natural gas, and coal assets. Talen set a significant market precedent by selling a portion of the Susquehanna nuclear plant campus in Pennsylvania to Amazon Web Services (AWS) for a direct-powered data center colocation facility — the first deal of its kind. While Talen lacks the scale to compete with Constellation for large national contracts, its pioneering data center colocation model has influenced the broader industry and may preview future deal structures.

### 4. Duke Energy (NYSE: DUK)

Duke Energy is the largest regulated electric utility in the United States, operating in regulated states (Carolinas, Florida, Indiana, Ohio) with significant nuclear capacity (~11 GW). Duke's nuclear fleet is embedded in regulated utility operations — meaning its earnings are guaranteed returns set by state regulators, rather than market-determined. Duke competes indirectly with Constellation for C&I customers in its service territory, but its regulatory model fundamentally differs from Constellation's competitive merchant approach. Duke is not a direct competitor for hyperscaler nuclear PPAs, as its nuclear assets are rate-based and cannot be contracted freely to third parties.

### 5. Southern Company (NYSE: SO)

Southern Company operates multiple nuclear plants, including the newly completed Vogtle Units 3 and 4 in Georgia — the first new nuclear reactors completed in the United States in decades. However, like Duke, Southern's nuclear assets are embedded in regulated utility operations and are not freely available for wholesale PPAs. Southern competes with Constellation primarily in C&I retail energy markets in the Southeast.

### 6. NextEra Energy (NYSE: NEE)

NextEra Energy is the world's largest generator of wind and solar energy and one of the most valuable utility companies in the United States. NextEra operates two nuclear plants (Seabrook in New Hampshire, Turkey Point and Point Beach in Florida) through its subsidiary NextEra Energy Resources — approximately 3.9 GW of nuclear capacity. NextEra competes with Constellation primarily for renewable energy C&I customers and in long-term clean energy PPA markets. While NextEra's renewable scale is unmatched, its nuclear exposure is substantially smaller than Constellation's, limiting its ability to offer the 24/7 carbon-free profile that hyperscalers increasingly demand.

### 7. Talen Energy, Lightpath Energy, and Emerging New Nuclear Entrants

A new category of competitor is emerging around advanced nuclear and small modular reactors (SMRs). Companies such as X-energy, TerraPower, Kairos Power, and others are pursuing NRC licensing for next-generation reactor designs. Google has contracted with Kairos Power for SMR capacity. Microsoft has contracted with Constellation for TMI restart power but also separately with TerraPower for future SMR capacity. While SMRs remain years from commercial deployment at scale, they represent a long-term potential disruption to Constellation's nuclear moat if they can deliver competitive economics.

2. **Geographic diversification**: Multi-RTO presence insulates earnings from any single market's price dynamics

## 8. MARKET OPPORTUNITY (TAM)

### Total Addressable Market: U.S. Competitive Power and Retail Energy

The broadest measure of Constellation's addressable market is the total U.S. electricity market, which produces approximately 4,100 terawatt-hours (TWh) of electricity annually and represents roughly $450–500 billion in annual revenue at blended wholesale and retail prices.

Within this, the competitive wholesale power market — where Constellation's generation assets sell electricity — represents approximately $150–200 billion in annual revenues. This is the primary revenue driver for Constellation's generation business.

The competitive retail energy supply market (deregulated residential, small commercial, and C&I customers who choose their electricity supplier) represents approximately $80–120 billion annually in the markets where Constellation is active.

### The AI Data Center Opportunity: The Critical Growth Vector

The most consequential growth opportunity for Constellation is the intersection of nuclear power and AI data center electricity demand. The numbers are staggering:

- **Current AI data center power demand**: Approximately 19–33 GW in the United States as of 2024–2025
- **2030 projected demand**: 35–120 GW (wide range reflects uncertainty in AI adoption rates and data center efficiency improvements)
- **2035 projected demand**: Up to 176 GW
- **Goldman Sachs estimate**: U.S. data center electricity demand rises 160% by 2030

Not all of this demand will be served by nuclear — renewables, natural gas, and grid improvements will also contribute. However, Constellation's 22.6 GW nuclear fleet is positioned to capture a disproportionate share of the premium, long-term, clean baseload power contracts that hyperscalers are seeking. Management has indicated that signed PPAs and Letters of Intent already represent over 5,650 MW of long-term clean energy commitments.

### Nuclear PPA Premium

The price premium that hyperscalers are willing to pay for long-term nuclear PPAs is a critical market dynamic. Recent deals have been struck at significantly above current market power prices — the Microsoft TMI PPA, for instance, is structured at prices that reflect the value of 24/7 carbon-free power, not just commodity electricity. These premium PPA prices expand Constellation's revenue opportunity well beyond what the underlying wholesale market would suggest.

### Nuclear Uprates

Approximately 1 GW of planned nuclear uprates at existing plants represents an additional $1–2 billion in potential annual revenue at current power prices, deployable at below-market capital costs (typically $500–1,000/kW) because the underlying civil and nuclear infrastructure is already in place.

### Government and Federal Market

The U.S. federal government is the world's largest consumer of electricity, with a mandate to achieve 100% clean electricity for its operations by 2030. Constellation's $1 billion federal buildings contract represents the opening chapter of what could be a multi-billion-dollar government energy services market — spanning power supply, efficiency services, and potentially onsite nuclear generation.

### Serviceable Market (Current)

Constellation's current footprint — 2.5 million retail accounts, $3.8 billion in long-term contract value, presence in PJM, MISO, NYISO, and ERCOT — represents a substantial portion of the competitive wholesale and retail markets. The Calpine acquisition expanded the serviceable market to California and additional Southeast markets.

Management projects strong free cash flow growth through 2029, supported by organic growth from existing assets, premium PPA additions, nuclear uprates, and the Calpine integration.

## 9. RISK ASSESSMENT

### Company-Specific Risks

**Risk 1: Calpine Integration Execution Risk**
The $16.4 billion acquisition of Calpine Corporation closed in January 2026, making it Constellation's largest-ever transaction. Integration of a company of Calpine's scale — approximately 26 GW of gas generation, thousands of employees, complex commercial contracts — is inherently difficult. Cost synergies and revenue benefits may take longer to materialize than projected, and integration errors could strain management attention, systems, and culture. 2026 EPS guidance of $11–$12 per share fell slightly below consensus expectations, partly reflecting integration cost headwinds. Severity: High. Likelihood: Moderate.

**Risk 2: Key-Person Risk — CEO Joe Dominguez**
Joe Dominguez is the defining figure of the Constellation narrative. His personal relationships with hyperscaler CEOs, his political and regulatory access, and his articulation of the nuclear-AI investment thesis are central to investor confidence and commercial deal flow. The departure, incapacitation, or reputational damage of Dominguez could materially affect both the stock price and Constellation's ability to execute the premium PPA strategy. Severity: High. Likelihood: Low.

**Risk 3: Nuclear Operational Risk — Unplanned Outages and Aging Fleet**
Constellation's 21 nuclear reactors have an average age of approximately 40 years. While U.S. nuclear plants have demonstrated strong operational performance and life extension is technically feasible, aging components require ongoing capital investment and create potential for unplanned outages. A significant unplanned outage at a large plant (such as Braidwood or Peach Bottom) could reduce generation volumes and result in market purchases at potentially unfavorable prices to fulfill contracted obligations. The NRC's rigorous licensing process provides assurance but also generates ongoing compliance costs. Severity: Moderate. Likelihood: Moderate.

**Risk 4: Hyperscaler Concentration Risk**
Microsoft and Meta represent two of Constellation's largest and most strategically important PPA customers — and both of these relationships are locked into 20-year terms. However, the concentration of premium revenue in a small number of hyperscaler relationships creates vulnerability to customer-specific events: a hyperscaler's reduced appetite for nuclear power (due to technology, policy, or strategic shifts), financial difficulties, or contractual disputes could materially affect Constellation's revenue outlook. Severity: Moderate. Likelihood: Low.

**Risk 5: Three Mile Island / Crane Restart Execution Risk**
The planned restart of the Crane Clean Energy Center (Three Mile Island Unit 1) in 2027 is a complex engineering and regulatory undertaking with no direct precedent in the United States. Delays in achieving restart (whether due to regulatory, engineering, or supply chain issues) would defer the contracted Microsoft PPA revenues and increase the project's capital cost. The PJM market monitor's opposition to certain FERC waivers related to the plant's capacity rights adds regulatory uncertainty. Cost overruns above the $1.6 billion estimate are possible. Severity: Moderate. Likelihood: Moderate.

**Risk 6: Customer Concentration in Retail**
While Constellation serves 2.5 million retail accounts, the C&I segment is dominated by a relatively small number of very large accounts. Loss of major C&I contracts — particularly in competitive procurement events — could reduce retail segment profitability.

### Industry/Market Risks

**Risk 7: Section 45U PTC Modification or Elimination**
The nuclear Production Tax Credit under Section 45U of the Inflation Reduction Act is the single most important policy driver of Constellation's earnings model. The credit provides up to $15/MWh for eligible nuclear generation but is subject to a profitability test (credits phase out as power prices rise). Political risk — including potential modification of the IRA in future Congressional action — could reduce or eliminate the PTC before its natural expiration. Analysts note that PTC roll-offs are expected to become a factor by mid-2027 as power prices rise above threshold levels. Any legislative change that reduces the PTC value would be directly negative for earnings. Severity: High. Likelihood: Low-to-Moderate.

**Risk 8: FERC Market Rule Changes**
FERC's oversight of organized wholesale electricity markets (PJM, MISO, NYISO, ERCOT) means that changes to market rules — capacity payment mechanisms, energy market design, transmission access rules — directly impact Constellation's revenues. The PJM capacity market has been subject to prolonged regulatory disputes over minimum offer price rules (MOPR) and Capacity Performance standards. Adverse rule changes could reduce capacity revenues that form an important part of Constellation's earnings base. Severity: Moderate. Likelihood: Moderate.

**Risk 9: Competition from Renewable Energy + Storage**
The continued rapid cost decline of solar, wind, and increasingly grid-scale battery storage creates long-term competitive pressure on nuclear's market share of electricity generation. While renewables cannot currently replicate nuclear's 24/7 carbon-free profile, continued technology progress (particularly in storage) could eventually narrow this gap. Hyperscalers that currently prefer nuclear for "always-on" clean power may have access to renewable-plus-storage alternatives by the 2030s that are cost-competitive with long-term nuclear PPAs. Severity: Moderate. Likelihood: Low (near-term), rising over longer horizon.

**Risk 10: Transmission Constraints and Grid Congestion**
Constellation's ability to deliver power from its nuclear fleet to end customers depends on transmission infrastructure that is often congested, particularly in PJM's Mid-Atlantic and Midwestern regions. Transmission constraints can reduce the realized prices that Constellation earns for nuclear generation and delay the delivery of power under PPAs. The delay in transferring capacity interconnection rights for the Crane restart (resolved by FERC waiver in June 2026) illustrates this risk. Severity: Moderate. Likelihood: Moderate.

### Financial Risks

**Risk 11: Post-Acquisition Leverage**
The Calpine acquisition added substantial debt to Constellation's balance sheet. While management has committed to a clear deleveraging path, elevated leverage reduces financial flexibility, increases interest expense, and makes the company more vulnerable to any earnings shortfall. Rising interest rates would increase the cost of any variable-rate debt and reduce the present value of Constellation's long-dated PPA cash flows. The company's credit rating (Baa1/BBB+ as of early 2026) provides investment-grade access to capital markets but leaves limited headroom before sub-investment-grade territory. Severity: Moderate. Likelihood: Low-to-Moderate.

**Risk 12: Power Price Sensitivity**
Despite the significant proportion of revenue under long-term PPAs and the 45U PTC providing a floor, Constellation still retains meaningful exposure to wholesale power prices for its "open" (uncontracted) generation volumes. A sustained decline in natural gas prices (which sets the marginal price of electricity in most hours) or a structural oversupply of renewable generation could compress wholesale prices, reducing revenues from unhedged nuclear output. Severity: Moderate. Likelihood: Moderate.

### Macroeconomic Risks

**Risk 13: Macroeconomic Slowdown Reducing Electricity Demand**
A significant U.S. or global economic recession could reduce industrial electricity demand, lower wholesale power prices, and reduce C&I retail customers' appetite for long-term energy contracts. Nuclear plants' high fixed costs mean that any reduction in generation output or revenue per MWh directly impacts margins. The current demand growth story (AI data centers) could also be disrupted if AI investment cycles slow materially. Severity: Moderate. Likelihood: Low-to-Moderate.

**Risk 14: Geopolitical Risk — Uranium Supply and Nuclear Fuel**
Nuclear fuel requires uranium enrichment and fuel fabrication services. The Russia-Ukraine conflict significantly disrupted global enrichment markets, as TENEX (Russia's state enrichment company) has historically supplied a significant share of U.S. nuclear fuel. Legislative restrictions on Russian uranium imports (signed into law in 2024) create near-term supply chain adjustment costs and potential fuel cost inflation. Constellation has diversified its enrichment supply chain, but any geopolitical disruption to uranium mining (concentrated in Kazakhstan, Canada, and Australia) or enrichment capacity could increase fuel costs. Severity: Low-to-Moderate. Likelihood: Low.
